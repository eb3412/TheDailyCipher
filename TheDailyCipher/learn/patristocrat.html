Patristocrat Cipher

Practice substitution cryptanalysis without the original spaces between words.

OVERVIEW

What it is

Patristocrat is recreational-cryptography terminology closely related to Aristocrat. Both use monoalphabetic substitution, but a Patristocrat removes the spacing that gives Aristocrat solvers valuable word-length clues.

That single formatting change makes cryptanalysis noticeably harder. Solvers must rely more heavily on repeated letter patterns, frequency information, probable fragments, and larger-scale language structure.

A monoalphabetic substitution cryptogram in which the original word boundaries are removed.
MECHANICS

How it works

Encrypt using one fixed substitution alphabet just as with an Aristocrat.

After substitution, remove the original word divisions. Ciphertext may then be grouped into uniform blocks only for readability.

Build or encrypt an example

1

Create a valid monoalphabetic substitution alphabet.

2

Substitute every plaintext letter consistently.

3

Remove the plaintext spaces.

4

Optionally regroup the ciphertext into equal-size blocks.

CRYPTANALYSIS

How to solve it

Without word boundaries, many of the easiest Aristocrat clues disappear.

Repeated sequences, global frequency, probable fragments, and reconstruction of likely word boundaries become especially important.

1

Count common letters and repeated sequences.

2

Search for likely digraphs and trigraphs.

3

Build tentative substitution mappings.

4

Look for places where likely word boundaries could occur.

5

Use emerging plaintext fragments to refine both the mapping and spacing.
