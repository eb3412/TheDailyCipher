Fractionated Morse Cipher

Follow plaintext through Morse code, separators, trigrams, and a keyed alphabet.

OVERVIEW

What it is

Fractionated Morse belongs to the recreational fractionation systems popularized through puzzle cryptography. It combines the familiar dot-and-dash structure of Morse code with a substitution stage.

The method is interesting because one ciphertext letter usually represents information spanning parts of multiple plaintext letters. That breaks the direct one-plaintext-letter-to-one-ciphertext-letter relationship found in ordinary substitution.

A cipher that converts plaintext into Morse code, groups the Morse stream into triples, and substitutes those triples through a keyed alphabet.
MECHANICS

How it works

Convert plaintext letters to Morse. Add separators between letters and stronger separators between words.

Divide the resulting dot-dash-separator stream into groups of three and map each group through a keyed alphabet.

CRYPTANALYSIS

How to solve it

With the keyword known, reverse the keyed substitution first.

The recovered trigrams reconstruct the Morse stream, which can then be divided at separators and translated normally.

1

Reconstruct the keyed alphabet.

2

Convert each ciphertext letter back to its Morse trigram.

3

Join the trigrams into one continuous stream.

4

Use separators to recover Morse letters and words.

5

Translate the Morse symbols to plaintext.

COMMON MISTAKES

What to watch for

Losing the separator symbols between Morse letters.
Grouping the original plaintext into threes instead of grouping the Morse stream.
Building the keyed alphabet incorrectly when the keyword contains repeated letters.
