Porta Cipher

See how a repeating keyword selects reciprocal substitutions from the Porta tableau.

OVERVIEW

What it is

The Porta cipher is named for the Renaissance polymath Giambattista della Porta, who wrote extensively about secret writing and cryptanalysis. The exact historical development of reciprocal polyalphabetic systems is more complicated than a single-inventor story; similar reciprocal ideas had appeared earlier.

For a modern solver, its most distinctive feature is reciprocity: applying the same transformation with the same key can reverse the encryption.

A reciprocal polyalphabetic cipher controlled by a repeating keyword and paired keyword letters.
MECHANICS

How it works

A repeating keyword selects one of thirteen reciprocal substitution alphabets.

Keyword letters are paired: A/B select one alphabet, C/D the next, continuing through Y/Z.

CRYPTANALYSIS

How to solve it

When the keyword is known, Porta decryption uses the same reciprocal operation as encryption.

When the key is unknown, repeated-key behavior and language statistics become useful.

1

Determine or obtain the keyword.

2

Repeat the keyword beneath the ciphertext.

3

4

Apply the same Porta substitution operation to recover plaintext.

COMMON MISTAKES

What to watch for

Treating Porta exactly like Vigenère addition.
Forgetting that keyword letters operate in pairs.
Advancing the keyword on spaces or punctuation when the convention says not to.
